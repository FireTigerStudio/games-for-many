Agar.io launched in 2015 and basically invented the .io genre. Eleven years later it still runs, but the servers are half-empty most hours, the leaderboards are bot-heavy, and anyone who spent 200 hours mastering split-shot combos back then is looking for something new. That's what this list is for – games like Agar.io that keep the core loop alive somewhere else.

Three rules for anything that made the cut. It has to run in a browser tab with no download, no account, no launcher. It has to share the grow-and-eat loop in some form – get bigger by consuming, get eaten by things bigger than you. And it has to be free. That's the bar.

Below are 10 picks ranked by how close they play to the original. #1 is the closest thing to Agar.io you'll find on the modern web. #10 keeps only the top-down survival feel. If you want the shortest answer possible, similar to agario means Cellular War, and everything else on this list gives you a different flavor of that same idea.

## What Makes a Game "Like Agar.io"?

Four mechanics define the original. First, your shape grows as you consume – whether that's cells, orbs, fish, or trails. Second, size determines the food chain: bigger eats smaller, no exceptions. Third, the arena is open and infinite-feeling with no walls dictating routes. Fourth, the skill floor is a mouse and the skill ceiling is deep – splitting, feeding teammates, ambushing at map edges.

Games similar to agar io keep at least two of those four. The ranking below is basically how many they preserve. Agar io similar games that hit 4/4 are direct clones; ones that hit 2/4 keep the vibe but bring their own twist. That's the sorting logic.

## Top Games Like Agar.io Ranked by Similarity

## 1. Cellular War: Online Multiplayer

Cellular War hits all four Agar.io mechanics without missing one. You steer a cell with the mouse, absorb anything smaller, run from anything bigger, and the arena stretches out under a microscope-lens aesthetic that leans harder into the biology angle than the original ever did. Round pacing is quicker – matches settle in around 6-8 minutes instead of 15.

Where it wins over Agar.io: no bots pretending to be players, cleaner UI, faster feed animation when you eat. Where it loses: no split-and-shoot. The advanced skill ceiling from 2015 Agar.io – ejecting mass to snipe a bigger cell, splitting to catch a runner – isn't here. If you spent hours mastering that, you'll feel the missing depth in your first match.

If you want the closest thing to Agar.io on the modern web, start here.

## 2. Greedy Snake Multiplayer Duel

Greedy Snake keeps three of the four core mechanics but swaps the ball shape for a snake. You still grow by consuming (orbs instead of cells), size still matters, the arena is still open, and encircling opponents replaces splitting as the main aggressive move. The head-hits-body kill rule means you're always doing the same size-vs-position math as in Agar.io, just from a different angle.

Chunky cartoon skin makes it feel less clinical than the original. Real flaw: no bot fill. Play at off-hours and you'll wait in an empty lobby for a minute before another human shows up. During normal hours it's fine. For the snake variant of this genre specifically, see [our full guide to Slither.io alternatives](/blog/games-like-slither-io/).

[Play Greedy Snake Multiplayer Duel →](/games/greedy-snake-multiplayer-duel/)

## 3. Snake War Multiplayer

Snake War is the harder-edged snake variant. Same grow-by-eating base as Slither.io, but killed snakes drop resource clumps anyone can vacuum up – which turns every kill into a mini-scavenge fight. That resource-drop mechanic is what pulls it a step closer to Agar.io's mass-ejection idea, since you can bulk up quickly by cleaning up someone else's death.

Loads faster than Slither.io itself, which matters if your connection is slow or you're on a school Chromebook. Honest flaw: it's very close to the Slither formula and doesn't do a lot the original doesn't. Play this one because it's here and it works, not because it invents anything new.

[Play Snake War Multiplayer →](/games/snake-war-multiplayer/)

## 4. Fish Eat Getting Big

Fish Eat Getting Big is Agar.io re-skinned as an ocean, with one feature no other agario like games on this list offer: three-player local support on one keyboard. Three input clusters share a single laptop – rare setup for browser io. Everyone on shared screen, ranks reshuffling every few seconds as sizes shift.

Ate anything smaller, ran from anything bigger, watched the leaderboard flip three times in a minute. That's the whole experience. The trade-off is real: three humans crammed onto one keyboard is physically cramped, and there's no online mode at all. If you and two friends are in the same room and want the Agar.io loop right now, nothing else on this list matches it.

[Play Fish Eat Getting Big →](/games/fish-eat-getting-big/)

## 5. Fish Eat Fish 2

The sequel to Fish Eat Fish splits into solo, 2-player, and 3-player modes on the same device. Growth advantage is drawn very clearly – bigger fish, bigger sprite, obvious visual read – so anyone new to the game figures out who they can chase in their first round. Good for mixed-skill sessions.

Same online-mode absence as its predecessor. It's essentially a near-duplicate of Fish Eat Getting Big on the same site, so pick one and stick with it – the sequel plays cleaner and loads faster, but the original has slightly better fish variety. Coin toss otherwise.

[Play Fish Eat Fish 2 →](/games/fish-eat-fish-2/)

## 6. Tung Sahur IO

Tung Sahur runs 100-player matches and turns the grow loop into infection spread. You start as a school kid running around a 3D map. Get caught by an infected player, become one yourself, join the horde chasing the survivors. Same "avoid bigger, chase smaller" math as Agar.io, just applied to numbers instead of size.

TikTok Bombardino Crocodilo and Tung Sahur meme characters carry the aesthetic, which means the whole game has a shelf life. When the brainrot memes fade, this one will feel dated fast. Right now it's a legit chaos machine and 100-player matches on browser are still uncommon. Play it while it's culturally alive.

[Play Tung Sahur IO →](/games/tung-sahur-io/)

## 7. Color Path IO

Color Path takes the risk-versus-greed spine of Agar.io and replaces eating with painting. Every movement drops a colored trail behind you. Close a loop and the enclosed area becomes yours. Bigger loops claim more, but a longer tail exposed on the field is an easier target for someone else to cut and kill.

You're not eating cells but you're doing the same fundamental math every second: how much do I push before I retreat? Player pool is smaller than Paper.io itself, so lobbies thin out at odd hours. For more territory-style games in the same lane, we've written up [territory .io games like Territorial.io](/blog/games-like-territorial-io/) as a full category breakdown.

[Play Color Path IO →](/games/color-path-io/)

## 8. Speen

Speen shares almost nothing mechanically with Agar.io – two circles linked together, one plants while the other swings around it, and you swap which one pivots with a single key. Momentum is your only weapon. But it's set in a shared multiplayer world with territory painting and PvP, and there's a grow element as you collect glow pickups to charge your leg.

Learning curve is real. The swap-pivot control takes maybe ten minutes to click and some players quit before that happens. Once it clicks, nothing else feels like it. This one's for players who mastered Agar.io years ago and want something totally new in the same lobby-based structure.

[Play Speen →](/games/speen/)

## 9. GrowWars.io

GrowWars is a two-player local arena where both heroes evolve through the match. WASD for one player, arrow keys plus a few extra keys for the other, and each side has movement, attack, and dash mapped independently. Combat-heavier than most agario like games – closer to a mini fighter than a pure eat-and-grow experience.

The evolution system is the Agar.io link: you're getting bigger and better mechanically as the match goes on. Real limit: no online mode, so both players have to be in the room. If they are, this is a stronger 1v1 pick than most local options on this list.

[Play GrowWars.io →](/games/growwars-io/)

## 10. Survev.io

Survev takes the top-down survival view that Agar.io uses and runs it through a battle royale template. You start with fists, loot buildings for guns and medkits, and a shrinking safe zone forces the final circle. The eat-and-grow loop is gone – replaced by loot progression – but the overhead perspective and the "run from the strong, hunt the weak" instinct is the same.

Graphics look dated compared to Fortnite. UI can feel busy when you're managing three medkits and two guns. Neither matters much when a match runs 8-12 minutes with no launcher. Good bridge if you want to evolve past Agar.io into something more combat-focused.

[Play Survev.io →](/games/survev-io/)

## FAQ

### Is Agar.io shutting down?

Not officially. It still runs at agar.io and Miniclip hasn't announced anything. What has changed is the community – active player counts are a fraction of the 2015-2017 peak, matches feel bot-heavy, and the top clans that used to run the leaderboards mostly moved on years ago. Looking for similar games to agar.io is a reasonable response to that, not a panic move.

### Which is the best Agar.io alternative for beginners?

Cellular War is the easiest starting point. Same mouse-to-steer control, same visual food chain (small cells are food, big cells are danger), no split-and-shoot mechanics to memorize before you can compete. Lobbies fill quickly during peak hours and matches wrap in under ten minutes, so a bad round doesn't cost you much.

### Can I play these games at school like I played Agar.io?

Yes. Every pick on this list is pure HTML5 with no client install, no plugin requirement, no port opening. That gets past most school and work network filters the same way the original Agar.io does. If your specific network blocks browser games as a category, no site can promise around that – [our list of unblocked .io games](/blog/unblocked-io-games/) breaks that down further.

### Are there Agar.io-style games with local multiplayer?

Two on this list support it. Fish Eat Getting Big runs three players on one keyboard with three input clusters. Fish Eat Fish 2 does solo, 2P, and 3P modes on the same device. Neither has an online mode, so they only work when your other players are physically in the room. GrowWars.io is a local 2P agar.io alternative if you want combat over pure eating.

### What about Slither.io or Diep.io – are those the same as Agar.io?

Same family, different branches. Slither.io is the snake variant where head-hits-body decides kills instead of size difference. Diep.io is the tank variant with ranged combat and upgrade trees on top of the base loop. If those are what you actually want, we've got dedicated lists: [Slither.io alternatives](/blog/games-like-slither-io/) and [Diep.io alternatives](/blog/games-like-diep-io/).
