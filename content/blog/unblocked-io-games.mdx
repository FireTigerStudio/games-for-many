## How These Games Stay "Unblocked"

These games run because they're pure HTML5. No client installs on the machine, no ports need opening, no launcher tries to phone home. School and work networks usually block Steam, Epic, and desktop apps – they don't always block a static webpage that happens to contain a game.

You'll also find io games mirrored on GitHub Pages. Search "unblocked games github io" and you'll hit a hundred subdomains hosting the same handful of titles. That works for some networks and gets blocked on others – it's not a guarantee. We don't run a github io games unblocked mirror ourselves.

One more thing worth the unblocked games tag distinction: not every website ending in .io is a .io game. The .io domain is just an Indian Ocean country code that game developers latched onto because Agar.io hit in 2015. It became a genre label, not a hosting rule.

## Snake & Slither .IO Games

The snake .io formula came out of Slither.io in 2016 and hasn't really left. You grow by eating pellets, kill by making other players crash into your body, and try not to do the same. Both games below use that base loop with different edges.

### Greedy Snake Multiplayer Duel

Greedy Snake is the closest slither.io clone in this catalog – head-hits-body kill rule, mouse-to-steer, orbs to eat, cartoon skin over the top. The whole point is length management. Coil too tight and someone slips through; sprawl too wide and you're an easy target to circle.

The chunky cartoon art makes it feel gentler than something like Survev.io, but the trap-and-encircle strategy is fully alive here. Where it falls short: no bot fill. If you jump in at 3am your time, the lobby is empty and you're staring at your own snake for a minute waiting for someone to join.

Best pick if you specifically miss Slither.io and want something one click away. For [more Slither.io-style games](/blog/games-like-slither-io/), we've got a dedicated list.

[Play Greedy Snake Multiplayer Duel →](/games/greedy-snake-multiplayer-duel/)

### Snake War Multiplayer

Snake War is the harder-edged version. Same slither base – eat orbs, grow, don't crash – but killed snakes drop resource clumps that anyone can vacuum up. That single change turns the whole game into a scavenging fight around dead bodies. Kills matter more, running away matters less.

It loads faster than the actual Slither.io site, which is a real advantage if you're on unblocked games slither io territory and every second of load time is a chance for a filter to catch it. Honest flaw: mechanically it doesn't do much you can't do in the original. Play this one because it's here and it works, not because it reinvents the genre.

## Grow-and-Eat .IO Games (Agar-Style)

Agar.io is the granddad of this whole category. The loop is simple: you're a blob, you eat smaller blobs, bigger blobs eat you. Three games on the site sit in this space, two of them local-only which is unusual for browser io.

### Cellular War — Online Multiplayer

Cellular War is the most direct agar.io clone here. Microscopic arena, cells absorbing cells, no signup, no split-and-shoot mechanics to learn. If you miss the core agar loop specifically – just steering a blob and watching a number go up – this is the fastest way back in.

The lack of splitting is the trade-off. Advanced agar players used split-shot combos as the whole skill ceiling; without them, matches settle earlier. If that's what you want, this is fine. If it's not, you'll bounce off in ten minutes. We've written up [our full list of Agar.io alternatives](/blog/games-like-agar-io/) if you want to compare.

[Play Cellular War →](/games/cellular-war-online-multiplayer/)

### Fish Eat Getting Big

Fish Eat Getting Big is agar in a fish skin, with one feature no online io game gives you: three-player local on one keyboard. Rare setup – most browser io assumes you're playing over the internet. Three players squeeze onto WASD, arrow keys, and a middle set, and the whole thing plays out on a shared screen.

Ranks reshuffle constantly because size changes constantly, so being ahead at the 30-second mark means nothing. The trade: no online option, and three players crammed onto one keyboard is physically cramped. Works best if you've got two friends actually in the room.

[Play Fish Eat Getting Big →](/games/fish-eat-getting-big/)

### Fish Eat Fish 2

The sequel splits into solo, 2-player, and 3-player modes on the same device. Growth advantage is visually loud – bigger fish, obvious control – so a new player can figure out who they can chase inside their first round. Good for a mixed-skill session where somebody hasn't played it before.

Same downside as its predecessor: no online play. If everyone in the group is remote, skip both fish games. If you're on one couch or around one laptop, this is the easier entry point of the two.

[Play Fish Eat Fish 2 →](/games/fish-eat-fish-2/)

## Territory & Painting .IO Games

Paper.io started this sub-genre in 2018. You leave a colored trail behind you, close a loop, and claim whatever's inside. The catch is your trail is a hitbox – opponents crossing it kill you.

### Color Path IO

Color Path is a clean take on that formula. Every movement drops a colored trail, bigger loops claim more area, but a stretched-out trail is easier for someone else to cut. Every second of play is a risk-versus-greed decision – close the loop early for a safe small gain, or push for a big claim and hope nobody catches your tail.

Player pool is smaller than Paper.io itself, so at off-peak hours the lobby feels quiet. During normal hours it fills quickly. For [more territory-style .io games](/blog/games-like-territorial-io/) beyond this one, we've done a full breakdown of the category.

## Battle Royale .IO Games

Two very different reads on the BR formula. One serious, one meme-driven.

### Survev.io

Survev.io is the most complete battle royale you'll find in a browser tab. Top-down 2D view, start with fists, loot buildings for guns, ammo, medkits, gear. Shrinking safe zone forces everyone into a final circle. No launcher, no download, no account – if you want the actual BR loop without installing anything, this is the pick.

Graphics look dated next to Warzone or Fortnite, and the UI can feel busy in loot menus. Nobody cares. The game plays cleanly and matches run 8-15 minutes, which is what a browser session actually needs.

[Play Survev.io →](/games/survev-io/)

### Tung Sahur IO

Up to 100 players, TikTok Bombardino Crocodilo characters, brainrot aesthetic bolted onto an infection game. You start as an innocent school kid. Get caught by an infected player, become one, spread it further. Chaotic in exactly the way it's trying to be.

The meme reference will age in about six months and the whole game will feel dated. Right now it's a genuinely fun 5-minute session if you're already deep in TikTok culture. If you have no idea what "tung tung tung sahur" is, this one isn't for you.

## Combat Arena .IO Games

Three duel-focused picks. Different weapons, different pace.

### Guardz.io

Close-range medieval sword combat. Knights swing, spacing decides the fight, crowded engagements kill you fast. Learning curve is picking up how far each swing reaches – get that wrong and you'll die in three seconds every time.

The one-note issue is real: it's melee only, no ranged option. If you played KnightFightIO and wanted more of that, you'll like Guardz. If you want variety in your combat, this isn't it.

[Play Guardz.io →](/games/guardz-io/)

### GrowWars.io

Local two-player arena where both heroes evolve mid-fight. One keyboard split down the middle, movement plus attack plus dash mapped per side. It's the most combat-heavy shared-keyboard game on the site – closer to a mini fighting game than an io duel.

No online mode means both players have to be in the room. That's a real limit. Everything else about it works.

[Play GrowWars.io →](/games/growwars-io/)

### Viking Tomahawk

Axe-throwing 1v1. CPU opponent or a friend on the same machine. Positioning and throw timing decide each round, and rounds finish in under a minute. Best-of-five plays out in five.

Skill ceiling is shallow – the fifth match plays a lot like the first. Fine for quick rounds, wrong pick for a long session.

[Play Viking Tomahawk →](/games/viking-tomahawk/)

## Fast-Movement & Physics .IO

One entry, because nothing else on the site plays like it.

### Speen

Speen has one mechanic and it's weird: two circles connected together, and you swap which one pivots to swing through the world. Momentum is the whole game. Collect glow pickups, charge a leg, smash spike balls, take out other players, then drop into the shared multiplayer world to paint territory and duel.

The swap-pivot control takes ten minutes to click and some players quit before it does. Once it clicks, the physics feels great – you're not moving a character, you're conducting momentum. If you're bored of standard steer-and-eat io mechanics, this is the outlier worth trying.

## FAQ

### Are these .io games actually unblocked at school?

They're built to work anywhere a modern browser works – HTML5, no client, no plugin, no port opening. That gets past most school and work filters. Some networks specifically block the unblockedgames.io style domains or entire game categories, and if yours does, no site can promise around that. GitHub Pages mirrors sometimes squeeze through where our main URL doesn't.

### Do I need to download anything to play?

No. Every unblocked games i.o title on this page loads in the browser tab you already have open. Chromebooks, older laptops, phones – if it can run Chrome or Safari, it can run these. There's no installer, no account signup, no email confirmation loop. Click the link, wait a couple seconds, you're in.

### What actually counts as a .io game?

A .io game is real-time multiplayer, jumps in fast, uses one or two controls, and finishes a round in a few minutes. That's the tag. The .io domain suffix started the trend when Agar.io launched, but plenty of io unblocked games don't use the .io domain anymore, and plenty of .io domains have nothing to do with the genre. It's a style, not a URL.
